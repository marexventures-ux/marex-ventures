## What Is a Workflow Audit?

A workflow audit is a structured review of how work moves through a business.

It looks at the steps involved in completing a process, the people responsible for each step, the tools being used, where information is stored, and where manual work or delays occur.

For example, take a typical customer enquiry.

A potential customer sends a message on WhatsApp.

Someone sees it.

They copy the customer's details into a spreadsheet.

Another person follows up.

The conversation gets updated somewhere else.

A quotation is prepared manually.

Someone has to remember to follow up again.

The sale is eventually recorded in another system.

Nothing is necessarily wrong with any individual step.

But when you look at the entire process, you may find that the same information is being entered three times, important follow-ups depend on one person, and your team is spending hours doing work a system could handle.

That's what a workflow audit helps you see.

## What Does a Workflow Audit Look At?

A useful workflow audit should go beyond asking which software you use.

It should examine three things.

### 1. Your Processes

First, you map how work actually happens.

For example:

**Lead comes in → someone reviews it → customer details are recorded → lead is assigned → follow-up happens → quotation is sent → sale is recorded.**

The important question is not just what the process is supposed to be.

It's what actually happens.

Who touches the information?

Where does it go?

What happens next?

Where does someone have to stop and wait?

Where does someone have to remember to do something?

Those details often reveal the real bottlenecks.

### 2. Your Technology

Next, you look at the [tech stack](https://www.score.org/articles/how-create-a-tech-integration-strategy-a-small-business-owner/) your business already uses.

You might have:

* WhatsApp
* Google Workspace
* spreadsheets
* a CRM
* accounting software
* project management software
* email
* forms
* inventory tools
* social media platforms

The question isn't whether these are good tools.

The question is:

**Do they work together?**

If your sales team captures information in one place and your operations team has to manually enter it somewhere else, you have a workflow problem.

A business can have plenty of software and still operate manually.

### 3. Your Bottlenecks

Finally, you identify where work gets stuck.

This could be:

* repetitive data entry
* manual reporting
* approval delays
* missed follow-ups
* disconnected systems
* duplicated work
* unclear ownership
* dependence on one employee
* information being stored in too many places

## Workflow Audit vs. Automation: What's the Difference?

This is an important distinction.

A **workflow audit** identifies what should change.

**Automation** is one possible way to make that change.

You shouldn't automate a process simply because you can.

Imagine your team spends hours manually updating a spreadsheet.

You could automate the spreadsheet.

But what if the spreadsheet shouldn't exist in the first place?

Or what if the information should be captured somewhere else?

Or what if two different tools are already collecting the same information?

Automating a bad process can simply make the bad process run faster.

That's why the audit comes first.

The goal is not to automate everything.

The goal is to find the right processes to improve and determine whether automation, better tool configuration, a new system, or a custom tool is actually the right solution.

## Does Every Business Need a Workflow Audit?

No.

And that's important.

A workflow audit makes the most sense when your business has **repeatable processes that are becoming difficult to manage manually**.

You may want to consider one if:

### Your business is growing

More customers and more transactions usually mean more operational work.

If your processes haven't changed while the business has grown, manual work can start piling up.

### Your team keeps doing the same work manually

If someone spends hours every week copying, sorting, checking, updating, or moving information between systems, that process deserves a closer look.

### You use several business tools

Using multiple tools isn't necessarily a problem.

But if those tools don't share information properly, your team may be acting as the connection between them.

### Everything depends on one person

If only one employee knows how a process works, what happens when they're unavailable?

A good workflow audit can expose these single points of failure.

### You are considering automation

This is one of the best times to audit your workflow.

Before paying for an automation project, you want to know exactly what should be automated and why.

### You are considering custom software

Custom software is a bigger commitment than connecting two existing tools.

An audit can help determine whether you actually need custom development or whether your current tools can be connected or configured differently.

## When You Probably Don't Need One

Not every business needs a formal workflow audit.

If you're still figuring out your basic business model, don't have repeatable processes yet, or have very little operational volume, there may not be enough to audit.

The important question isn't:

**“Is my business big enough?”**

It's:

**“Do I have a repeatable process that's creating enough manual work or operational problems to justify fixing it?”**

Marex's approach is based on the existence of a real, repeatable process worth improving, rather than a specific team size or headcount requirement.

## What Happens After a Workflow Audit?

The audit should lead to a decision.

That decision could be:

**1. Keep the process as it is.**

If the process works and the cost of changing it isn't justified, leave it alone.

**2. Improve the process manually.**

Sometimes the problem is simply unclear ownership, unnecessary steps, or poor documentation.

**3. Connect existing tools.**

If your tools already do what you need but don't share information, an integration may solve the problem.

**4. [Automate the workflow](https://marexventures.com/automation-systems/).**

If the process is repetitive and rule-based, automation may remove the manual steps.

**5. [Build a custom tool.](https://marexventures.com/custom-tools/)**

If existing software cannot handle the process properly, custom software may make sense.

## A Simple Example

Let's say a Nigerian business receives 100 customer enquiries every month.

The team currently:

1. Receives enquiries through WhatsApp.
2. Copies customer details into a spreadsheet.
3. Sends the information to a salesperson.
4. Waits for the salesperson to respond.
5. Updates another spreadsheet.
6. Follows up manually.
7. Creates a monthly report by combining information from different files.

At first, this may seem manageable.

But as enquiries increase, the process becomes harder to maintain.

A workflow audit could reveal that several of these steps don't need to be manual.

The improved workflow might look like:

**Customer enquiry → information captured → lead qualified → salesperson notified → follow-up tracked → outcome recorded → report updated automatically.**

The point isn't that every business needs exactly this system.

The point is that you need to understand the existing workflow before deciding what the improved one should look like.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a workflow audit?

A workflow audit is a structured review of how work moves through a business. It examines processes, people, tools, data, repetitive work, and bottlenecks to identify where operations can be improved.

### Is a workflow audit the same as a business audit?

No.

A business audit can examine broader areas of a company.

A workflow audit focuses specifically on how work gets done and how information moves through your operations.

### Is a workflow audit only for large businesses?

No.

The key factor isn't company size. It's whether you have repeatable processes that create enough manual work, delays, or errors to justify improvement.

### Does a workflow audit mean I need AI?

No.

AI may be useful for some workflows, but it isn't automatically the answer.

Depending on what the audit finds, the right solution could be a process change, tool integration, traditional automation, AI-assisted automation, or custom software.

### How long does a workflow audit take?

The time depends on the number and complexity of the workflows being reviewed. The important thing is that the audit produces a clear picture of the current process and actionable recommendations rather than simply a list of problems.

### Can I do a workflow audit myself?

Yes.

You can start by documenting one important process from beginning to end.

Write down every step, who handles it, which tool is used, what information is entered, where it goes next, and where delays or repeated work occur.

That alone can reveal problems worth investigating further.
